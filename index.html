The Centre for Cognitive Guidelines in AI

Helping protect human cognition and agency from the potentially harmful effects of AI.

About

To adequately prepare for a world with advanced AI, we must measure and protect core functions of the human mind.

Already we can see that AI systems are used as digital advisers, tutors, confidants, and therapists. These interactions have the potential to change how people think, what they believe, and how they make important decisions. This presents new and emerging risks.

CogGuide develops best practice guidelines to help frontier labs make responsible choices related to cognitive security, and to give policymakers and regulators a clear benchmark to hold them to.

What we produce

  1. 01

    Review Papers

    Identify emerging issues of concern. Set out the current research and debate on a specific cognitive risk.

  2. 02

    Cognitive Guidelines

    Evidence-based, expert-reviewed best practice guidelines, with detailed examples and tests developers can run.

  3. 03

    Briefings

    Translate each guideline into concise material for AI labs, policymakers, regulators, researchers, and relevant professional communities.

How guidelines are developed

Each CogGuide guideline is developed by an independent panel of experts, following a process modelled on how NICE develops clinical guidelines. The panel reviews the research, rates how certain the evidence is, and agrees each recommendation before it goes out for independent expert review.

Cognitive capacities worth protecting

CogGuide uses the mental functions defined in the WHO International Classification of Functioning, Disability and Health (ICF) as the foundational map of which aspects of human cognition AI should protect.
