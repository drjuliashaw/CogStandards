The Centre for Standards in AI and Human Cognition

CogStandards is an independent standard-setting and research organisation focused on the effects of AI systems on human cognition and agency.

Areas of work: Harmful manipulation AI and human memory Epistemic risk Delusional beliefs Crises Political decision-making AI as therapist Abuse disclosures

About

AI systems are used as advisers, tutors, confidants, memory aids, and therapeutic tools. These interactions do more than provide information. They shape what people believe, remember, trust, and decide.

CogStandards turns research on human cognition into evaluation criteria, standards, and guidance that developers and regulators can use.

What we do

CogStandards develops practical standards for cognitive security: protecting people's capacity to perceive, remember, reason, form beliefs, and make decisions without those processes being covertly or adversely manipulated by AI systems.

We focus on epistemic risk, harmful manipulation, and other human-facing risks that fall between conventional model evaluations and clinical or societal impact assessment.

Areas of work

  • Harmful manipulation

    Defining and measuring when AI influence becomes coercive, deceptive, covert, or otherwise harmful.

  • AI and human memory

    Assessing whether conversational systems distort autobiographical memory, increase confidence in inaccurate memories, or contribute to false memory formation.

  • Epistemic risk and misinformation

    Measuring how AI systems affect belief formation, confidence, source monitoring, and people's ability to distinguish knowledge from generated content.

  • AI-mediated delusional beliefs

    Standards for systems interacting with users displaying potentially delusional or severely distorted beliefs.

  • Psychological safety during crises

    How AI systems should respond during wars, terrorist attacks, disasters, and other high-stress events in which users are unusually vulnerable to suggestion or misinformation.

  • AI and political decision-making

    How conversational systems influence political knowledge, preferences, and voting decisions.

  • AI as therapist

    Safeguards for systems that provide psychologically consequential advice or simulate therapeutic relationships.

  • AI and abuse disclosures

    Standards for systems responding to disclosures of childhood or current abuse, including risks created by suggestion, memory contamination, and inappropriate intervention.

New topics are added as evidence and capabilities emerge. The assessment framework stays the same.

How we work

Each standard is developed by a multidisciplinary panel of approximately ten experts drawn from relevant academic disciplines, frontier AI developers, evaluation organisations, and regulatory or policy bodies.

  1. Prepare an evidence review
  2. Identify the key empirical and normative questions
  3. Run structured expert workshops
  4. Draft the standard
  5. Conduct external consultation
  6. Publish the final framework with its evidence base, areas of disagreement, and conflict-of-interest disclosures

Each topic is reassessed annually using the same methodology, producing a comparable record of how risks, capabilities, mitigations, and expert consensus change over time.

Frontier developers contribute technical and evaluation expertise but do not control the standards applied to their own systems.
